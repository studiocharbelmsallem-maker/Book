Couture week is a room that has already seen everything. A Paris image is not shot to please; it is shot to survive an editor with forty other collections open on her desk.

I · Fashion show

The Courtyard

Haussmann interiors, then the courtyard show
Not a gown. A presence. Five women enter Paris in sculpted light.
Why it works  A show image has one job: to make the collection read as a single idea before it reads as pieces. We shoot the courtyard show in open daylight, with the next looks queued in soft focus behind the first, so the eye reads the sequence of the collection in one frame. Sunlight is honest with fabric: silk keeps its sheen, construction keeps its edges, and the front row's phones in the frame say the moment was real.

II · Editorial

The Château

A gown in the company of stone that has waited longer
One silhouette, a château across the water, a grey sky that flatters silk.
Why it works  An editorial is the frame that puts a house on a page instead of in a feed. Northern light is soft and directional, so texture survives on print and on a phone. We keep the palette in one cool family, stone, water, sky, so nothing in the frame competes with the dress, and the embroidery becomes the only detail the eye returns to.

III · Casual

Off Duty

The register that humanises a house
The same city on a Tuesday: a beret, a white collar, a column at her back.
Why it works  Couture earns respect; ready-to-wear earns desire. The casual register shows the woman the collection was cut for, living in it. We shoot it in passing daylight against architecture, so the clothes feel worn rather than styled, and we keep the tones earthy and close so the image reads as one breath in the first second of a Reel.
