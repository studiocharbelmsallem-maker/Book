Selected workChapter 01

Beirut

Where the light is learned

A city that keeps losing its electricity and never its standard. Beirut is where this studio learned to make art under pressure and under war — to decide instead of decorate: one source, one hour, no second day. Over two decades most of the city's houses have come through this lens, many of them more than once. What a designer inherits from that training is a director who arrives with the frame already resolved, a crew that does not need the room to be easy, and nothing left for the designer to manage.

Dubai

Where the collection is wanted

The Gulf does not ask a gown to be quiet. This is appetite at scale — and a client who buys with her eyes before she reads a single word. Every frame here had to survive two distances: a phone held at arm's length, and a print three metres wide. Same negative, both rooms.

Paris

Where it is judged

Couture week is a room that has already seen everything. Nothing in this chapter was shot to please a client; it was shot to survive an editor with forty other collections open on her desk. This is the register that puts a house on a page instead of in a feed — and the reason the studio is in Paris three times a year.

Milano

Where it is sold

Buyers do not fall in love first. They calculate, and then they fall in love. Milano is commerce with taste: imagery that has to hold in a showroom, on a linesheet, and above a runway without being reshot for each. The frames that turn a compliment into an order.

Istanbul

Where it crosses

East and west arguing politely, in silk. Ottoman interiors, European tailoring, and a bridal tradition that answers to both at once. This is the proof a collection can be directed to read in two markets without apologising to either — the chapter that matters most to a house selling from Riyadh to Milan.

Ottoman interiors and Byzantine stone. The studio's longest single narrative.

Jewellery

Where the eye rests

The hardest discipline in the studio. A stone has no silhouette to hide behind, no wind, no walk, no fabric to fall. Only light, hand and intent. Shot so the piece reads as an object of desire rather than a product on a page.
