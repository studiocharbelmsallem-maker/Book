I · Bridal
The Staircase
Wedding shot in open daylight rather than a studio
A veil that falls the length of a stone stair, in morning light.
    
Why it works  Romance without sentiment is a matter of restraint. We let the architecture carry the grandeur and keep the bride still, so the veil and the staircase draw the same long line. Morning light on warm stone keeps the white soft and the skin true.

II · Editorial
The Pavilion
Modernist stone at Mediterranean noon
Black and ivory volume against carved busts and garlands.
    
Why it works  A strong silhouette needs a strong setting. Shot from below, the gown rises against carved stone and the composition becomes graphic, black against ivory against sand. Noon light is unforgiving, so we use it only where the shape is bold enough to take it.

III · Evening
The Colonnade
Two frames, silver against stone
Crystal fringe and blue sequins walking along a row of columns.
    
Why it works  Evening wear is about movement and catch-light. Low sun along the colonnade turns every fringe and crystal into a moving highlight as she walks, and the warm columns frame the cool blue of the gown. It is the frame that sells the dress before anyone touches it.
